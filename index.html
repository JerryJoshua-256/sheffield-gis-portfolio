01 / OVERVIEW

A spatial approach to urban security.

The project evaluated how proposed CCTV schemes could improve surveillance coverage in Sheffield, with particular attention to areas experiencing higher reported crime and to conservation areas. The work was undertaken during a SIWES programme, using QGIS to guide a Sheffield University Urban Studies and Planning student through the project.

02 / BRIEF

The planning question

The client brief focused on evaluating surveillance schemes under a limited budget, comparing the overall coverage of service areas and the distribution of cameras in relation to high-crime LSOAs.

Plan A

Install 10 new CCTV cameras

Each proposed camera has a 2 km range. The stated rationale is to maximise coverage in risky areas with no current camera presence.

Plan B

Upgrade 10 existing cameras

Upgrade selected existing cameras to advanced models, increasing their monitoring range from 2 km to 3 km. The stated rationale is to enhance coverage in high-crime-density areas.

04 / METHOD

From incident points to priority areas

The documented analysis combines attribute selection, location-based selection, buffer analysis, intersection, field calculations and attribute joins.

Aggregate crime by LSOA

Use Select by Location to count crime points within each LSOA and create a crime-count layer. The methodology section also describes aggregation using Join Attributes by Location.

Map crime incidents

Generate a choropleth map of total crime incidents by LSOA for September 2023.

Select high-incident LSOAs

Use the ≥30 monthly incident threshold. The report identifies 41 such LSOAs, representing at least one recorded crime per day during the 30-day period.

Buffer existing CCTV

Create a 2 km buffer around each existing CCTV point and dissolve the buffers into one coverage geometry.

Identify CCTV service areas

Intersect the CCTV coverage geometry with LSOA polygons to identify the portions of LSOAs reached by the buffers.

Calculate coverage measures

Calculate Area_Ha, Covered_Ha and %covered for each intersected LSOA portion.

Restore the full LSOA layer

Join the intersected layer back to the original LSOA layer using the unique lsoa21cd field, retaining LSOAs not touched by a buffer.

Classify priority

Apply the documented expression using monthly crime count and percentage of existing CCTV coverage to classify five priority levels.

Assess Plan A

Assess coverage from the 10 proposed new cameras, each with a 2 km range, against the priority categories.

Assess Plan B

Generate a 3 km buffer for the 10 proposed camera upgrades and assess coverage against the same priority categories.

Compare the alternatives

Compare coverage by priority class and overall coverage, and consider the spatial distribution of proposed cameras.

Consider conservation areas

Review the locations of proposed and existing cameras in relation to conservation area polygons.

05 / EXPRESSION

Priority classification logic

The following is the expression reproduced from the project report.

CASE
WHEN "Crime_Coun" >= 30 AND "% Covered" = 0
THEN 'VERY HIGH'
WHEN "Crime_Coun" >= 30 AND "% Covered" > 0 AND "% Covered" <=50
THEN 'HIGH'
WHEN "Crime_Coun" >= 30 AND "% Covered" > 50
THEN 'MEDIUM'
WHEN "Crime_Coun" <30 AND "% Covered" = 0
THEN 'LOW'
WHEN "Crime_Coun" < 30 AND "% Covered" > 0
THEN 'LEAST'
END
The report's category logic is retained as written. The expression assumes the field names shown in the report are present in the working layer.

08 / DECISION

Why the report selected Plan A

Although Plan B has the greater overall coverage in the report's interpretation, the final decision favours Plan A.

Final decision: Plan A. The report gives three reasons:

  1. It covered various areas around the city, not just centralised CCTV coverage.
  2. Its focus on Low Priority Areas was intended to help prevent the spill of crime into those areas.
  3. Even where CCTV coverage already exists around the city centre, higher crime rates were still experienced in those regions compared with outer regions. The report therefore argues for preventing the spread of more crime to Low and Least Priority areas rather than adding more cameras to already-covered areas.
09 / CONSERVATION

Conservation-area considerations

The report notes that some proposed cameras in both alternatives were sited within a few conservation areas, while existing cameras were also located within or at the edges of some conservation areas. It emphasises due caution in planning activities affecting these designated areas.

The source describes a UK conservation area as an area designated by a local planning authority for special architectural or historic interest, whose character or appearance is desirable to preserve or enhance, citing the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990, section 69.

10 / TECHNICAL WORKFLOW

Reproducible QGIS sequence

This workflow follows the operations documented in the report. It does not add unreported parameters, coordinate systems, classification breaks or processing settings.

Project setup

  1. Open QGIS and create a project for the Sheffield security planning analysis.
  2. Load the Sheffield LSOA 2021 polygons, Sheffield boundary, September 2023 crime points, crime-count CSV, conservation area polygons, existing CCTV points, and Plan A and Plan B installation layers.
  3. Confirm that the LSOA code field lsoa21cd is available in the LSOA and intersected data.

Crime aggregation and mapping

  1. Use Select by Location to identify crime points within each LSOA and create the crime-count layer, as described in the project detail.
  2. The methodology summary also records aggregation using Join Attributes by Location; retain this distinction when documenting the original process.
  3. Generate a choropleth map of total crime incidents per LSOA.
  4. Use Select by Expression or attribute selection to identify LSOAs with crime count greater than or equal to 30.

Existing CCTV service-area analysis

  1. Run a 2 km Buffer on existing CCTV point locations.
  2. Dissolve the individual buffers into one coverage geometry.
  3. Run Intersect between the dissolved coverage and the LSOA polygons.
  4. In the intersected attribute table, calculate the LSOA area, covered area in hectares and percentage covered. The report names the fields Area_Ha, Covered_Ha and %covered.
  5. Run Join Attributes by Field Value to join the intersected layer to the original LSOA layer using lsoa21cd, preserving LSOAs with no CCTV-buffer intersection.

Priority classification and alternatives

  1. Apply the exact CASE expression reproduced above using the crime-count and percentage-covered fields.
  2. Summarise the number and area of LSOAs in each of the five priority classes.
  3. Assess Plan A using the proposed 10 new cameras and their 2 km coverage range.
  4. Assess Plan B using a 3 km buffer for the 10 existing cameras proposed for upgrade.
  5. Compare each alternative's covered area and percentage across the priority categories and overall.
  6. Overlay or visually compare camera locations and conservation area polygons, then record the planning considerations.
The PDF does not specify the QGIS version, CRS, exact choropleth classification method or class breaks, field-calculator formulas, buffer segment settings, or exact Plan A/Plan B processing sequence beyond the operations described. These have intentionally not been invented.
